1. Emotional Intelligence & Self-Awareness

What is Emotional Intelligence?

Emotional Intelligence (EI) is the ability to recognize, understand, and manage emotions in oneself and others, and to use that awareness to guide thought and behavior.

Emotional Intelligence (EI)Ability to perceive, understand, regulate emotions in self and others for effective decision-making and relationships.
Self-AwarenessAccurate understanding of one's emotions, strengths, weaknesses, values, motivations.
Social AwarenessAbility to read others' emotions, empathize, understand social dynamics.
Self-RegulationManaging emotional responses, impulse control, adaptability.

Key Components of Self-Awareness

  • Emotional Awareness: Recognize own emotions as they occur
  • Accurate Self-Assessment: Realistic view of strengths, weaknesses
  • Self-Acceptance: Accepting self including imperfections
  • Self-Confidence: Trust in self-worth and capabilities
Why EI Matters
  • ✔ Builds stronger relationships and teamwork
  • ✔ Improves communication and conflict management
  • ✔ Better decision-making (logic + emotion)
  • ✔ Leadership, resilience, stress handling

2. Understanding & Managing Emotions

Foundation

Emotions are complex psychological and physiological states triggered by stimuli, influencing thoughts, behavior, relationships.

Primary EmotionsInstinctual, universal, automatic responses: fear, anger, sadness, joy, surprise, disgust.
Secondary EmotionsComplex emotions from primary + thoughts: guilt, shame, jealousy, pride, anxiety.
Emotional LabelingNaming emotions precisely (e.g., anxious vs. "bad") to reduce intensity.
Body ScanNoticing physical signals: racing heart, trembling, stomach knot as emotional cues.

Managing Emotions - Core Strategies

Emotional RegulationControlling intensity and duration of emotional response.
Deep BreathingSlow inhale through nose, hold, slow exhale to calm nervous system.
ReappraisalReinterpreting threat as opportunity for growth.
Emotional ExpressionHealthy outlet: journaling, art, music, talking to trusted person.
Quick Framework - R-I-D-E

Recognize: "I'm feeling angry"
Identify source: Why?
Decide strategy: Breathe / Reappraise
Express: Constructively

3. Empathy & Perspective Taking

EmpathyFeeling with someone, acknowledging and validating their emotions to build trust. Different from sympathy (feeling sorry for).
SympathyFeeling sorry for someone from a distance.
Perspective TakingMental exercise of seeing situation from another's point of view: thoughts, feelings, motivations.

Three Components of Empathy

Affective EmpathyFeel emotions alongside others.
Cognitive EmpathyUnderstand another's thoughts and perspective.
Compassionate EmpathyDesire to help alleviate suffering, action-oriented.

Developing Empathy - Active Listening

  • Maintain eye contact, open body language
  • Ask open-ended questions
  • Paraphrase: "So what you're saying is..."
  • Pick up nonverbal cues
  • Ask: "What would I feel if I were in their shoes?"

4. Effective Communication

Exchange of information, ideas, feelings that is understood by all parties.

Effective CommunicationClear, concise message + active listening + appropriate response.
ClaritySimple language, no jargon, direct message.
Nonverbal CommunicationBody language, facial expression, tone, gestures — must align with words.
Active ListeningFully focusing, maintaining eye contact, clarifying.
FeedbackConstructive response that clarifies understanding.

Key Elements

  • Clarity: Short, specific, easy to understand
  • Nonverbal: Consistent eye contact, posture, tone
  • Empathy: See from other's perspective
  • Active Listening: Listen to understand, not to reply
  • Feedback: Open to criticism, encourage input
Benefits
  • ✔ Prevents misunderstandings and conflicts
  • ✔ Builds trust and rapport
  • ✔ Improves teamwork and collaboration
  • ✔ Enhances personal and professional relationships

5. Verbal & Nonverbal Strategies

Verbal CommunicationUse of words, spoken or written, to convey message.
Nonverbal CommunicationCommunication without words: gestures, posture, facial expression, tone, proxemics.

Verbal Strategies

  • Use simple, concise language
  • Be specific and direct
  • Adjust tone and pace to audience
  • Use "I" statements: "I feel..."
  • Avoid filler words

Nonverbal Strategies

  • Maintain appropriate eye contact
  • Open posture, nodding to show engagement
  • Mirror to build rapport (subtle)
  • Control facial expressions to match message
  • Mind proximity and personal space

6. Active Listening & Conflict Resolution

Active ListeningFull attention to speaker, understanding message beyond words.
Emotional ConflictConflict driven by feelings, values, perceptions rather than facts.
Difficult ConversationHigh-stakes discussion with differing opinions and strong emotions.

Techniques for Active Listening

  • Give undivided attention, minimize distractions
  • Use verbal affirmations: "I see", "Go on"
  • Paraphrase and summarize
  • Ask clarifying questions
  • Withhold judgment, observe nonverbals

Types of Emotional Conflicts

  • Value Conflicts: Differing beliefs, ethics
  • Relationship Conflicts: Poor communication, mistrust
  • Interest Conflicts: Competing needs, resources

Conflict Resolution Strategies

  • Prepare: clarify goal, anticipate emotions
  • Analyze situation from multiple perspectives
  • Use collaborative approach: win-win focus
  • Regulate emotions during conflict: pause, breathe
  • Self-reflection after: what worked, what to improve

7. Teamwork, Collaboration & Cultural Competence

TeamworkCooperative effort of group to achieve common goal.
CollaborationWorking jointly, sharing knowledge and skills.
DiversityDifferences in culture, ethnicity, gender, perspective, experience.
Cultural CompetenceAbility to understand, respect, and work effectively across cultures.
InclusionCreating environment where all members feel valued and able to contribute.

Principles of Effective Teamwork

  • Clear roles and responsibilities
  • Shared goals and accountability
  • Open communication and trust
  • Respect for diversity
  • Constructive feedback

Overcoming Barriers

  • Stereotyping and bias — challenge assumptions
  • Language barriers — use simple language, visuals
  • Communication styles — adapt to direct vs indirect
  • Build resilience through debriefs and learning from failure

8. Personal Values & Core Values

Personal ValuesCore beliefs and principles that guide behavior, decisions, life choices.
Core ValuesFundamental, deeply held values that form identity and purpose.

Why Values Matter

  • Guide decision-making and priorities
  • Provide sense of purpose and direction
  • Influence relationships and career choices
  • Foundation for motivation and resilience

Examples of Values - Vertical List

Personal ValuesAutonomy, Creativity, Integrity, Respect, Honesty, Growth, Balance, Responsibility
Relationship ValuesEmpathy, Loyalty, Communication, Trust, Compassion, Support, Understanding
Career ValuesChallenge, Recognition, Teamwork, Work-Life Balance, Leadership, Service, Innovation, Stability

Discovering Core Values - Steps

  1. Reflect on peak experiences and what made them meaningful
  2. Identify moments of anger/frustration — what value was violated?
  3. List 10 values that resonate, then narrow to top 5
  4. Define what each value means to you in action
  5. Test alignment: Are daily actions matching values?

9. SMART Goals & Goal Setting

Goal SettingProcess of identifying desired outcomes and creating plan to achieve them.

SMART Acronym - Vertical List

S - SpecificClear, precise, answers What, Why, Who, Where. Not vague. Example: "Improve public speaking" → "Deliver 5-min presentation without notes to 20 people"
M - MeasurableQuantifiable criteria to track progress. How much? How many? Example: "Write 500 words per day"
A - AchievableRealistic given resources, skills, time. Challenging but possible.
R - RelevantAligned with values, long-term vision, and career purpose. Why does it matter?
T - Time-boundDeadline, timeframe, milestones. Example: "By Dec 2026" or "3x per week"

Common Pitfalls

  • Goals too broad or vague
  • Unrealistic or unachievable
  • No clear deadline
  • Not aligned with values
Example - SMART in Practice

Vague: "I want to be healthier"
SMART: "Walk 30 minutes, 5 days per week, for next 2 months to improve cardio"

10. Personal Vision & Aligning Values with Goals

Personal Vision StatementConcise declaration of future self, purpose, and what you want to achieve and become.
Values-Goals AlignmentEnsuring goals reflect and support core values to sustain motivation.

Key Elements of Vision Statement

  • Future-oriented, inspiring
  • Clear values embedded
  • Defines impact and legacy
  • Concise (1-2 sentences) but meaningful
  • Action-oriented

Examples - Vertical

  • "To use creativity and empathy to empower youth through education and become a trusted mentor"
  • "To live with integrity, grow continuously, and build solutions that serve community with balance"
  • "To lead with respect and teamwork, achieving career growth while maintaining work-life balance"

Steps to Align Values with Goals

  1. List top 5 core values
  2. Write long-term vision (3-5 years)
  3. Break vision into SMART goals
  4. Check each goal: Which value does it serve?
  5. Adjust or drop goals that conflict with values
Practical Alignment Example

Value: Creativity + Growth
Step 1: Research local art classes
Step 2: Sign up for class
Step 3: Purchase supplies
Step 4: Commit 2x per week
→ Goal supports value

11. Time Management & Prioritization

Time ManagementPlanning and controlling how time is spent to increase effectiveness and productivity.
PrioritizationOrdering tasks by importance, urgency, deadlines.

Prioritization Techniques - Vertical Lists

Eisenhower Matrix (Urgent-Important)

Q1: Urgent & Important - Do FirstCrises, deadlines, emergencies. Example: Exam tomorrow, sick family member
Q2: Important but Not Urgent - SchedulePlanning, growth, relationships, prevention. Example: Exercise, studying, vision planning
Q3: Urgent but Not Important - DelegateInterruptions, some emails, meetings. Example: Non-essential calls
Q4: Not Urgent & Not Important - EliminateTime wasters, excessive social media, trivia

ABCD Method

A - Must DoMost important, serious consequences if not done
B - Should DoImportant, mild consequences
C - Nice to DoNo consequences, adds value
D - DelegateCan be done by someone else
E - EliminateRemove altogether

MoSCoW Method

Must-HaveCritical: Book venue, hire caterer
Should-HaveImportant but not critical: Send invitations, plan decorations
Could-HaveDesirable: Hire photographer, live band
Won't-Have (this time)Out of scope: Fireworks, photo booth

12. Productivity, Distractions & Procrastination

Personal ProductivityEfficiency in completing meaningful tasks with quality.
Productivity MetricsMeasures: tasks completed, time focused, quality, energy levels
DistractionStimulus that diverts attention: notifications, noise, multitasking
ProcrastinationDelaying tasks despite knowing negative consequences, often due to fear, perfectionism, overwhelm

Factors Affecting Productivity

  • Environment: clutter, noise, lighting
  • Energy: sleep, nutrition, exercise
  • Mindset: motivation, self-talk, clarity
  • Tools: unclear goals, too many tools, poor planning

Strategies to Boost Productivity

  • Set clear daily top 3 tasks (MIT)
  • Single-task, not multitask
  • Work in energy peaks (morning for most)
  • Use 2-minute rule: if <2 min, do now

Managing Distractions

  • Turn off non-essential notifications
  • Create dedicated workspace
  • Use website blockers during focus blocks
  • Batch emails and messages

Minimizing Procrastination

  • Break complex into smaller tasks
  • Use Pomodoro Technique
  • Set 5-minute start rule
  • Reward progress, not just completion
  • Self-compassion over self-criticism

13. Advanced Scheduling: Time Blocking & Pomodoro

Time BlockingScheduling day into blocks dedicated to specific tasks or types of work.
Pomodoro TechniqueWork 25 min focused, 5 min break, repeat. After 4 pomodoros, longer 15-30 min break.

Pomodoro Schedule - Vertical

Round 125 min Focus → 5 min Break
Round 225 min Focus → 5 min Break
Round 325 min Focus → 5 min Break
Round 425 min Focus → 15-30 min Long Break

Advanced Scheduling Strategies

  • Theming days: Monday - admin, Tuesday - creative, etc.
  • Buffer blocks: 15-30 min between meetings for overflow
  • Energy blocking: Deep work in high energy, shallow in low
  • Implementation: Plan next day evening, review morning
Example Schedule

8:00-8:30 Planning (Q2)
8:30-10:30 Deep Work - Study (Pomodoro x3)
10:30-10:45 Break
10:45-12:00 Collaboration / Meetings
13:00-14:30 Skill Practice
14:30-15:00 Buffer / Reflection

14. Mindfulness, Resilience & Stress Regulation

MindfulnessPresent-moment awareness without judgment — thoughts, emotions, sensations observed.
ResilienceAbility to bounce back from adversity, adapt, grow through challenges.
Growth Mindset (Pag-unlad ng Isipan)Belief abilities can be developed through effort, learning, persistence. Opposite of fixed mindset.
Fixed MindsetBelief abilities are static, effort is futile.

Pag-unlad ng Isipan - Key Concepts (from LMS Tagalog)

  • Konsepto ng Katatagan: Ability to withstand pressure, recover
  • Growth Mindset Practices: Magkaroon ng growth mindset, Magtrabaho nang mas mahusay, Huwag matakot sa pagsubok, Magtiwala sa sarili
  • Paano Magkaroon ng Pag-unlad: Seek feedback, learn from failure, celebrate effort not just result, reflective journaling

Stress & Emotional Regulation

StressBody's response to demand or threat — can be eustress (positive) or distress (negative).
EustressMotivating, short-term stress that improves performance
DistressOverwhelming, chronic stress that harms health
  • Mindfulness meditation 5-10 min daily
  • Body scan and breathing
  • Cognitive reappraisal
  • Social support and self-compassion
Mindfulness-Resilience Link

✔ Mindfulness increases awareness → better regulation → resilience. Resilience enables sustained growth mindset.

15. Creative Problem Solving & Decision Making

Creative Problem SolvingFinding novel, useful solutions beyond conventional approaches.
Decision MakingChoosing among alternatives based on values, information, consequences.

Creative Problem Solving - Steps

  1. Clarify problem: What is root cause? Reframe
  2. Generate ideas: Brainstorm without judgment, quantity over quality
  3. Develop solutions: Combine, refine, test feasibility
  4. Implement and evaluate: Try, reflect, iterate

Decision Making Tools

  • Pros-Cons list with values weighting
  • Decision matrix for multiple options
  • Consider long-term vs short-term impact
  • Consult diverse perspectives
Quick Check - 4 Questions

1. Does it align with core values?
2. What are risks and opportunities?
3. What would I advise a friend?
4. Can I live with this decision in 1 year?

16. Leadership Communication & Feedback

Leadership CommunicationClear, inspiring, accountable communication that aligns team toward vision.
FeedbackInformation about performance to guide growth — can be reinforcing or corrective.

Leadership Communication Techniques

  • Use simple, concise language, avoid jargon
  • Storytelling to inspire and contextualize
  • Transparency about why and what
  • Regular check-ins, not just annual reviews
  • Model active listening

Feedback Techniques - Vertical

SBI ModelSituation → Behavior → Impact. Example: "In meeting (S), you interrupted (B), team felt unheard (I)"
FeedforwardFocus on future improvement, not past blame
Reinforcing FeedbackAcknowledge strengths specifically to repeat
Corrective FeedbackAddress gap, offer support and clear next step

Overcoming Barriers

  • Fear of conflict — reframe as growth opportunity
  • Technology overload — choose right channel (face-to-face for sensitive)
  • Being open to feedback and willing to learn

17. Overcoming Obstacles & Long-Term Momentum

ObstacleBarrier that hinders progress — internal (fear, doubt) or external (resources, time).
SetbackTemporary reversal of progress, opportunity to learn.
MomentumSustained motion toward goals through consistent small actions.
Grit & PerseverancePassion + persistence for long-term goals despite difficulty.

Strategies to Overcome Obstacles

  • Break complex problems into smaller manageable tasks
  • Seek support, mentorship, guidance
  • Practice self-care and self-compassion
  • Reframe failure as data: "What did I learn?"
  • Develop contingency plans

Maintaining Long-Term Momentum - Steps

  1. Identify long-term objective — clear vision
  2. Break into smaller tasks — monthly, weekly milestones
  3. Establish timeline — realistic deadlines
  4. Create tracking system — journal, app, habit tracker
  5. Review and adjust — weekly review

Creating Supportive Environment

  • Join community or group related to goal
  • Find mentor or accountability partner
  • Eliminate distractions (notifications off)
  • Establish consistent routine or schedule
  • Celebrate small wins and accomplishments
Growth Loop

✔ View challenges as opportunities → Analyze failures → Growth mindset → Plan for obstacles → Self-compassion → Sustain motivation
